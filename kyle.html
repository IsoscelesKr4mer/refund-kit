The line he should not have typed

kes935 · 10:51 AM
"if you want to proceed that route, please don't expect to have any future friendly transactions. i take these situations very seriously."

That is a seller telling a buyer there will be consequences for using eBay's dispute process. It is a separate problem from the cancellation and it is worth reporting on its own, in its own sentence, so it does not get buried inside the refund complaint.

Report it as its own item, not as colour. "The seller warned me not to expect future transactions if I involved eBay" is a clean, quotable sentence. Do not editorialise around it. The quote does all the work and anything added to it weakens it.

Step three — what not to do

3

Three things that would cost you the win

Do not tell him you will drop the report if he sells you the box. Saying "I would still like the box, otherwise I will report this" was fine, and you already said it. Making the report conditional on the sale is not, because he refunded you and no longer owes you a sale. He would screenshot it and report you, and a store his size knows exactly how.

Do not canvass the Whatnot rooms. One factual post about your own experience is yours to make. Going room to room telling people not to buy from him is organised and repeated, which is the shape that gets called a campaign, and it is the one version of this that can be turned around on you.

Do not add the jab. Quoting his "as a grown adult" line back at him in a message feels excellent and converts a documented complaint into two men arguing. Save it for a post, where it works on an audience rather than on the one person who can still say yes.

What this is actually worth

Honestly: eBay will not make him ship it, and you are not getting that box at $899.99. He has told you he will not sell it to you and the price is now $1,749.99.

What a report can cost him is a transaction defect, which feeds seller level rather than the public feedback percentage. You cannot move the 100%, because cancelled orders cannot be rated, which you already found when the feedback button was not there. Enough defects and he loses Top Rated status, and Top Rated Plus is a 10% discount on final value fees. On a store doing that volume it is a meaningful monthly number, and a far bigger lever than a tenth of a percent of feedback.

And you have four more coming, all under a grand. That is worth holding on to. You are not short of the box, you are short of this box at this price, which means you can file this cleanly, let it go, and lose nothing either way.
